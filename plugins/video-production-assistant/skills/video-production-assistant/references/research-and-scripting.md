# Research and scripting (before recording)

## vidIQ research (optional)
Calls use the user's vidIQ credits. Say how many calls you plan (usually 3-6) before starting.
1. Keyword research on the topic: search volume, competition, rising queries. Pick the title wording people actually search.
2. Outliers and trending videos on the topic: which videos beat their channel's average, and what their titles, length and openings have in common.
3. Transcripts of 2-3 of the strongest videos: how fast they get to the point, what structure they use, what they leave out.
4. Top viewer questions from comments: these become sections or an FAQ chapter.

Report in plain English: 3-5 findings, each with what it means for this video. Do not copy other creators' scripts; use the research to choose angle, order and questions to answer.

## Script structure for training videos
- **Hook (0-20 s):** what the viewer will be able to do by the end, and why it matters to them.
- **Context (short):** the problem, in one or two sentences.
- **Steps or chapters:** one idea per chapter; follow the natural number of steps (3 is often enough). Each chapter: say it, show it, check it.
- **Recap (20-40 s):** the steps in one breath, and where to go next.
- Length follows content. Cut any sentence that does not teach, show or motivate.

## Shot list
For each script line, mark one of: `A` (to camera), `SCREEN` (screen recording; note what is on screen), `GFX` (title, lower third, callout - note the text), `DIAGRAM` (flat or 3D), `BROLL` (other footage - note source and rights). Save as a table in `script.md`. This becomes `timeline.json` after the edit.

## Monthly review (when the user asks)
With vidIQ: pull the user's last 5-10 videos' stats (views, retention where available, view velocity) and compare with the channel average. Find patterns: openings, lengths, topics or formats that did better or worse. Propose at most five changes to `edit_rules.md` or to the script structure, each tied to the evidence. Apply only those the user approves. With few videos, say the evidence is thin rather than over-read it.
